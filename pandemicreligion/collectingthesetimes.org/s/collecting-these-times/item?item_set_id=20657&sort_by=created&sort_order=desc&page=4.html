  • New Voices Magazine: Illustrating the Day-to-Day, "Young Jewish, and Alive" through COVID-19

    An illustration series where Rebecca Lubow reflects on moments during the pandemic. The drawings depict Zoom meetings and moments in isolation.

  • New Voices Magazine: Diaspora English, On A Year of War, Plague, and Turning

    Scholar Daniel Crasnow writes his final essay in the magazine's series "Diaspora English" on his experience teaching english in Tel Aviv and Nazareth. He reflects on his views on the Israeli/Palestine conflict and its evolvement during the pandemic and heightened tensions in the region.

  • Svara Article: Dedicating Our Learning to a New Malkhut

    An article written for Svara's section 'Hot off the Shtender'. This article was written by Laynie Soloman.

  • Svara Article: The Radical Act of Becoming a Talmud Person

    An article posted to the 'Hot off the Shtender' section of Svara's website. The article was written by Benay Lappe

  • Svara article: Practicing Freedom in the bet Midrash

    An article written for Svara's 'Hot off the Shtender' section of their website. This article was written by Laynie Soloman.

  • Svara Article: Toward A Co-Created Liberatory Jewish Future

    An article written for Svara's Hot off the Shtender, by Ayana Morse.

  • Dancing the Lulav Shuffle

    Wilshire Boulevard Temple's religious school reopened with a celebration of Sukkot, which included Rabbi David Eshel leading the socially-distanced Lulav Shuffle dance! Photo/video caption on Instagram: We’re throwing it back to opening day of @wbtreligiousschool this past Sunday, because who wouldn’t want to learn the Lulav Shuffle from our very own @rabbidavid37? There is no better way to celebrate Sukkot than by shakin that Lulav! To learn more about our amazing religious school program, make sure to follow their account or head to our link in bio. #lulavshuffle

  • Reboot Ideas: The Lost Art of Connecting

    Journalist Laurie Segall interviews Susan McPherson, writer of "The Lost Art of Connecting: The Gather, Ask, Do, Method for Building Meaningful Business Relationships". Their conversation covers the book's main themes on technology and the necessity of forging value-based relationships.

  • Judaism Unbound's Digital Afikoman Hunt

    Judaism Unbound offers a group activity that those celebrating Passover can do with their family and loved ones. The activity includes a word search that the group can do in order to find a secret URL leading to the Afikoman.

  • Elul Unbound 2020: Podcast Episode #9 - Elul Now

    Lex Rofeberg of jewishLIVE and Wendie Lash launch the first podcast celebrating the last month of the Jewish year, Elul. They interrogate the question: "why is this Elul different from all other Eluls?"

  • T'ruah: The Wisdom of Destroying Worlds

    Rabbi Avi Strausberg offers a d'var Torah fo Parshat Noach. She describes the story of Noah and how God chose to destroy the world and how important it is to find wisdom in the ruins. Connecting this to the effects that the COVID-19 pandemic has had on people in the last year and a half and she urges finding wisdom in the ruin.

  • Preparing for the High Holidays During a Pandemic

    President and CEO of The Jewish Federation of Greater Los Angeles talks with prominent rabbis, including Rabbi Sharon Brous of IKAR, about the many ways that Jewish Americans are preparing for the high holidays in the midst of a pandemic. They focus on the creative ways that the Jewish community across the country are reimagining the traditions of the holidays.

  • Shabbat Alone, Together

    The company OneTable offers a few ideas for celebrating Shabbat during the COVID-19 pandemic such as virtual Shabbat with family or a "shelter-in-place Shabbat".

  • JewishLIVE: Resource Hubs for Digital Seders for Passover 2020

    JewishLIVE compiled a list of resources for celebrating Passover digitally during the pandemic. Some of the resources included are recipes and links to scheduled events.

  • The Plagues Project: Teklit Michael and Elliot Glassenberg, a Refugee's Perspective

    Activists Teklit Michael and Elliot Glassenberg talk about the plagues and refugees in Jewish history and how the pandemic has impacted modern-day refugees.

  • The Plagues Project: Nathaniel Berman, Standing Between the Living and the Dead, Plague and the Incense Ritual in Kabbalah

    Scholar of Jewish mysticism Nathaniel Berman looks at Jewish tradition and the spiritual responses to the plague.

  • The Plagues Project: Richard Elliott Friedman, How Many Plagues Were There?

    Scholar of the Hebrew Bible Richard Elliott Friedman looks to the Torah in thinking about humanity during the pandemic.

  • Resources from Prizmah's Enrollment, Development, Finance and Budget Section

    Resources regarding enrollment, fundraising, funding, and other found on Prizmah's website intended to be seen by Jewish Individuals

  • The Plagues Project: Erin Leib Smokler, Theological to Vertigo

    Erin Leib Smokler talks about the life of Sarah, the trials she went through and how they relate to living near death and living in a plague.

  • The Plagues Project: Shira Hecht-Koller, Crafting Order from Chaos

    The second episode of jewishLIVE's The Plagues Project. Shira Hecht-Koller talks about how we can look to finding order in the chaos brought up during the pandemic.

  • IKAR: Rabbi Sharon Brous, Living in the Plural

    Rabbi Sharon Brous offers a sermon for Yom Kippur.

  • IKAR: Rabbi Ronit Tsadok, From Deluge to Dove

    Rabbi Tsadok offers a sermon for Rosh Hashanah. She speaks of the struggles of isolation brought on by the pandemic and how the strength of Noah's story can help guide us through these trials.

  • IKAR: Rabbi David Kasher, Searching for Faith

    Rabbi David Kasher offers a sermon on the second day of Rosh Hashanah.

  • IKAR: Rabbi Sharon Brous, The Still, Small Voice

    Rabbi Sharon Brous offers a sermon for the first day of Rosh Hashanah 2021. She talks about the importance of starting the year with a focus on stillness and gratefulness.

  • IKAR: Just Getting Started

    Founder of IKAR Rabbi Sharon Brous reiterates the purpose of IKAR, not only as a spiritual community but a community of activists who want to be voices of change.
