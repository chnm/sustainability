Collaborators



Initiated by NPR's Lost & Found Sound (The Kitchen Sisters – Davia Nelson and Nikki Silva), the Sonic Memorial Project is a national collaboration with NPR, WNYC, KQED, Picture Projects, dotsperinch, the September 11 Digital Archive Project, the Museum of Television and Radio, ABC News, transom.org, Creative Time, and more than 50 radio producers.

Numerous individuals contributed their time and resources to help make this important and living document of this time, these places, and these people.

The Kitchen Sisters Productions
Lost & Found Sound Radio Series
"From our work collecting archival recordings for the Lost & Found Sound radio series, we've come to realize how much even a simple phone message can mean. We've also come to know how quickly these fragile, ephemeral 'accidental documentaries' are lost and erased."

— Davia Nelson and Nikki Silva
The Kitchen Sisters, Davia Nelson and Nikki Silva, have been creating radio stories together over the past twenty years. They are the executive producers with Jay Allison of the Peabody Award winning radio series Lost & Found Sound that has been airing on NPR's All Things Considered over the past three years. The stories in this eclectic and sound-rich series explore the history of recorded sound and its impact on the last 100 years, including "The Rise & Fall & Rise & Fall & Rise of Thomas Alva Edison," "Portrait of an Artist as an Answering Machine," "WHER: 1000 Beautiful Watts-The Nation's First All-Girl Station," and "Cigar Stories: El Lector, He Who Reads." The project has produced more than 80 stories, three hour-long broadcast specials, an award-winning website and a two-CD compilation: "The Best of NPR's Lost & Found Sound, Vol. I" and has involved more than 800 people and institutions in its collaborative efforts.

Following the events of September 11, 2001, the Lost & Found Sound Team organized The Sonic Memorial Project, a collaboration involving NPR and producers across the country dedicated to gathering and preserving the sounds and stories of the World Trade Center and surrounding neighborhood.

Lost & Found Sound’s Sonic Memorial Project is produced by The Kitchen Sisters, Davia Nelson and Nikki Silva, with Jay Allison (curator of the Lost & Found Sound phone line), Jamie York (New York coordinator), Joe Richman, Ben Shapiro, Laura Folger, Elinoar Astrinksy, Grace Kee-Heifetz, Kate Volkman, and independent producers nationwide. Mixed by sound designer, Jim McKee, Earwax Productions.



Picture Projects
"We wanted to create a memorial without the strictures of height, length, or width—a home for memories of buildings that no longer exist. It is a place where all can mourn, honor, remember, and contribute; a place to keep history alive."
Since 1995, Picture Projects has been combining photography, audio, and the interactive capabilities of the web to create new forms of oral history, documentary, and journalism. Cofounders Alison Cornyn and Sue Johnson have collaborated with artists, writers, radio producers, and photographers to develop award-winning websites and interactive exhibitions including 360degrees.org: Perspectives on the US Criminal Justice System, akaKURDISTAN, a virtual archive of Kurdish history; and Re: Vietnam—Stories Since the War.

SonicMemorial.org is a continuation of Picture Projects' history of weaving new technologies with smart design into an ongoing exploration of our social fabric through participatory storytelling spaces.

dotsperinch—architects for emerging digital cultures

Founded by Mark Shepard and Carlos Tejada in 1998 as an experimental practice for architecture, film and new media, dotsperinch has grown into a network of artists, architects, technologists, and theorists working collaboratively on the development of exhibitions, installations, kiosks, and websites.

Partners and clients span the arts, museum, and education communities, including UNICEF, the National Building Museum, Ralph Appelbaum Associates, and the Irish Arts Council. Recent projects include 360degrees.org, the National Building Museum's virtual exhibit Building America, and the UNICEF Video Booth located @ 3 UN Plaza in New York City.
